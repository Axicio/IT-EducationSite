    How to Learn Coding Consistently

    Motivation fades. Here's how to build a coding practice that runs on habit instead.

    +420 views

    Most people don't quit learning to code because it's too hard — they quit because they relied on motivation, and motivation runs out. The learners who keep going treat coding practice less like an inspired activity and more like brushing their teeth: something that happens on a schedule, whether or not they feel like it.

    Anchor Practice to Something You Already Do

    Attaching a new habit to an existing one makes it far more likely to stick. "After my morning coffee" or "right before bed" works better than "whenever I have time," because "whenever I have time" quietly never arrives.

    Track Showing Up, Not Hours

    A streak of "did I open the editor today" is a better metric than "how many hours did I code," because it optimizes for the habit itself rather than for burning yourself out in one long session and skipping the next three days.

    Lower the Bar on Hard Days

    Some days, five minutes is all you've got — and five minutes still counts. Protecting the habit matters more than protecting the intensity. For a deeper look at this, our How to Build a Study Habit podcast episode covers the same idea with a few more concrete tactics.
